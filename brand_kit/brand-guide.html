The story

Who it's for: a student about to graduate who wants the best-paid AI job. Voice: plain, short, and talking to "you."

BeatAskLearn
OpeningWhat does the AI job market reward most?Our goal, three questions and the data.
1. Choose your focusDoes your skill, role or industry change your starting pay?Barely. The differences could be chance.
2. Choose your placeDoes where you apply change it?Yes. It can double your starting pay.
3. Build experienceHow does pay grow with experience?It nearly triples.
Your pathWhat should you do?Experience pays most, and place comes second.

Charts

  • Title the chart with its finding, not its topic.
  • Make one mark orange and the rest gray.
  • Line up bar values on the right.
  • Start bars at zero, and use one scale across charts people compare.
Ordered groups, such as entry to executive

#A3ABC2, #7782A0, #4A5677, #232D4B

Career lines: navy for the chosen place, gray for all countries, an orange dot for the chosen year. Label lines directly.

Gray and peach look too similar to stand for two categories. Never use color alone: add a label.

Same facts everywhere

Use these numbers as written so our three deliverables agree.

TopicSay this
AnswerAcross all jobs, experience explains 60% of pay variation, country 22% and company size 4%.
FocusSkill, role and industry explain under 1% of entry-level pay. Best-to-worst gaps ($4k, $7k, $6k) match random chance.
PlaceSwitzerland's entry-level pay ($94k) is 2.1 times Japan's ($46k). Country explains 66% of entry-level pay, company size 12%.
ExperienceMedian pay rises from $60k (entry) to $178k (executive), or 2.9 times.
Data15,000 synthetic postings from 20 countries, January 2024 to April 2025. Source: Bisma Sajjad, Kaggle (CC0).

Always include: "* This is a synthetic dataset created for educational purposes to simulate AI job market patterns. All data is algorithmically generated based on industry research and market trends."

Skip these

  • Gradients, shadows, 3D charts and pie charts.
  • Extra colors or fonts.
  • UVA logos or seals. We use the school colors only.
  • More than one orange highlight in a chart.
